The opportunity

A robot exception is never just a robot problem.

It can affect food quality, customer promises, ingredient readiness, staffing, maintenance, and the product teams improving the fleet. The interface must coordinate those consequences without turning a kitchen worker into a technician.

01

Detect

A dispensing path falls outside recipe tolerance while live orders continue arriving.

02

Protect

The system finds alternative capacity, predicts guest impact, and recommends a service-saving move.

03

Recover

Operators get a safe, simple machine action while support context accumulates automatically.

04

Learn

The event becomes a fleet signal and a possible preventive policy—not another lost ticket.

Human in the loop

Design system + IA

A system is more than components. It is a shared operational language.

The big design-system work is making hierarchy, signal strength, terminology, and meaning consistent across physical work, robot controls, manager tools, support, and engineering—without forcing every role to speak like the same product.

Make it real

Validate in the kitchen, not in a conference room.

The design is only useful if it reduces cognitive and physical load in an active service environment.

01

Observe peak service

Shadow kitchen operators, prep staff, managers, and technicians during a lunch rush to map real workarounds, handoffs, and time pressure.

02

Test simulated exceptions

Measure first-glance comprehension, time-to-correct-action, and whether a worker can explain what remains safe and productive.

03

Scale a design system

Document shared status, action, task, machine-state, and evidence patterns so teams can ship consistent experiences across products.
