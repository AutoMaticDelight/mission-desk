Peak-hour
service command.

A glove-first operating interface for robot-assisted kitchens. It turns live robot telemetry, order flow, inventory, demand forecasts, and service history into immediate, role-specific action.

One system.Kitchen operations, robot control, service recovery, prep, management, support, and fleet learning.
Design goal: people should not troubleshoot a robot during lunch rush. They should protect service, recover safely, and make the exception useful to the next shift and fleet.
The opportunity

A robot exception is never just a robot problem.

It can affect food quality, customer promises, ingredient readiness, staffing, maintenance, and the product teams improving the fleet. The interface must coordinate those consequences without turning a kitchen worker into a technician.

01

Detect

A dispensing path falls outside recipe tolerance while live orders continue arriving.

02

Protect

The system finds alternative capacity, predicts guest impact, and recommends a service-saving move.

03

Recover

Operators get a safe, simple machine action while support context accumulates automatically.

04

Learn

The event becomes a fleet signal and a possible preventive policy—not another lost ticket.

Prototype

Make the right operational move obvious.

The command surface leads with the customer outcome, then presents a single coordinated action. Technical evidence remains available, but it is never the operator’s primary job.
