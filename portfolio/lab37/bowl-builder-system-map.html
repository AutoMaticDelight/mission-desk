Platform architecture concept

One machine.
Many touchpoints.

Bowl Builder is not one interface. It is a connected kitchen production system: food prep, loading, live order assembly, quality capture, packaging, handoff, recovery, service, and fleet learning.

Design opportunity

Use one operational language across physical modules and digital surfaces, while adapting the controls, evidence, and terminology to the person responsible for the next action.

5. Taxonomy strategy

Make the system precise without making people speak robot.

The design-system problem is semantic as much as visual: the system needs a precise canonical data model, but each role should see language that helps them act.

Design system rule

Numbers are operational signals, not decoration.

Use size, weight, and contrast to make the most urgent decision readable at distance. Use color as a secondary signal with a stable semantic meaning: lime for the primary action, amber for attention, green for verified/normal, blue for information, red only for stop or safety-critical states.

Then pair every number with a noun the role understands: 7 orders at risk, 18 servings left, 36F safe, 00:27 build time, 99.2% pass rate.

The opportunity is to create a shared operating language across people, physical modules, recipes, orders, quality evidence, and the fleet—not merely a better dashboard.

Why this matters

This system map demonstrates that the design work can begin with object models, operational roles, and state semantics—then scale into on-machine UI, kitchen tools, service workflows, and fleet intelligence.

It also creates a practical design-system foundation: components, tokens, terminology, and interaction patterns connected to real kitchen work.
